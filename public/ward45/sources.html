Sources & method

Everything here comes from the City of Cape Town Open Data Portal unless stated. Data was pulled on 2026-11-17; "updated" is the date the City last modified each dataset.

How the numbers were worked out

  • Ward 45 and Subcouncil 19. The 2026 ward boundary and the City's subcouncil layer. Sampling 978 points across Ward 45 put 956 in Subcouncil 19; the rest fall outside any subcouncil polygon. A map layer counts for Ward 45 if it touches the ward boundary, so features on the edge are included.
  • Tables without coordinates (fire incidents, crashes, air quality, recyclers, licences, valuations) cannot be tied to the ward boundary. They are grouped by suburb or police precinct instead, and suburb names were cleaned by hand (for example "LAVENDERHILL" and "Lavender Hill East" both count as Lavender Hill).
  • Crash locations. Each crash record names a road and area. Names were matched to the City's road centrelines; route numbers (such as M4) were limited to the suburb in the record; intersections are placed where two named roads come within about 200 m. About 81% of records (11,136 of 13,720) matched a road; the other 19% appear in the charts only.
  • Air quality stations have no coordinates in the data. They are placed at the centre of the suburb they are named after, so distances are approximate. The nearest station is Athlone at 14.5 km.
  • Water quality. E. coli counts (CFU and MPN) are combined and shown as monthly geometric means. The 130 and 1,000 per 100 mL lines are indicative reference values for recreational contact (DWAF 1996). They help read the data; this is not a compliance assessment.
  • Population over time. The portal has one population estimate (2025). Home values and the number of residential properties from five valuation rolls stand in for change over time.
  • Budget figures come from the City's published budget annexures (2023/24 Annexure 38 and 2024/25 Annexure 33), not the portal.

Known limits

  • Fire incident records thin out sharply from 2019 to 2022 and look incomplete; do not read that as fewer incidents.
  • The ward boundary does not follow suburb lines, so suburb totals can't be added up to a ward total.
  • Marina da Gama is not an official planning suburb. It is shown where the City lists it separately (population, fire records, and the residents' association boundary).
  • Some layers are served from the City's QA server, which may differ from the production data.
  • The household survey is a sample; small areas have wide margins of error.

Tools

Maps use Leaflet with the Esri World Street Map basemap, with OpenStreetMap data; charts use Chart.js. The pages load these libraries and map tiles from the internet, so they need a connection to display. All report data is stored in the data folder next to the pages.
