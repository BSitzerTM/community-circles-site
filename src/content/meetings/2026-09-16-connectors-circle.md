**Street updates.** Bournemouth Road: Corli and Elaine are drafting an introductory message for their street group. Dartmouth cul-de-sac: Charles has answered questions and will meet Brad to finalise the integration.

**Brand and style guide (Corli).** Goal: a modular, DIY-focused brand identity that feels like a community rather than a formal organisation. Principles: replicable by hand (for example with a cookie cutter or thick marker), and modular, with swappable elements such as the wordmark, icon and place name. Full name: "Connected Community", to avoid the corporate feel of an acronym like CCME. Proposed elements: a blocky, stencil-like wordmark, cut out by hand and scanned for a "wonky" handmade feel, and a wave-shaped icon that resembles several Cs and can also be a smile or rainbow. Feedback: Brad suggested connecting the letters in the wordmark, and noted that the icon resembles a copyright symbol. Paula praised the DIY approach, which works for stencils and stamps. Next step: invite community members, including teenagers, to create icons for specific circles, such as Repair Cafe and Foam Circle.

**Website adaptations (Brad).** Brad will send a one-page request to developer Alwyn for a website login feature to store private documents, such as fundraising proposals and the brand guide. Tech stack debate: Corli proposed moving from Google Drive to a community-owned, open-source server for better control of data. Naomi and Paula are comfortable with familiar tools such as Google Drive because they are easy to use for less tech-savvy members. Brad acknowledged the trade-off between convenience and ethics. He suggested that Tech4Good, which launches on 23 September, could explore both: a parallel, accessible Google Drive and a more secure, open-source server.
